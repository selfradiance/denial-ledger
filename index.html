What gets in

  • A public figure, campaign, company or government office says a specific video, audio clip or photo is fake or AI-made.
  • The claim and what came out afterward are documented by at least one credible news organization or fact-checker, linked on the entry.
  • Both directions count. Fakes correctly called fake are logged right alongside real footage called fake. A ledger of only one side would be a hit list, not a record.
  • Each entry is rated by what the evidence says about the footage, on a scale from confirmed real to confirmed fake. "Likely" means credible analysis points one way but it isn't settled. "Unresolved" means the evidence doesn't settle it.
  • No private individuals. Claims are paraphrased, not quoted at length.
  • This is a sample, not a complete list.

Sealed, and why

Each entry file is fingerprinted (SHA-256) and the fingerprint is timestamped in the Bitcoin blockchain through OpenTimestamps. Your browser checks every entry against its seal when this page loads. If anyone edits a sealed entry, including us, it shows up as broken.

Mistakes don't get erased. They get a correction, which is sealed too and shown under the original. Wrong stays wrong, in public.

Check a seal yourself

Download an entry's file and its .ots seal from the links on the entry, then drop both into opentimestamps.org. It shows the Bitcoin block and date, and doesn't depend on this site.
